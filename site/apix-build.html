When a market prices dynamically, when you look decides the inflation number you print.

Right now that choice is implicit. APIx makes it explicit, auditable and reproducible — a daily matched-model price index for Indian domestic airfares, built to a standard the National Statistical Office could actually consume. This page is the build room: what exists, what each decision was, and what is still open.

What exists

Build state

The index is the deliverable; the scraper is plumbing. Effort went to the statistical layer first, which is why the hardest part is finished and the riskiest part is still being verified.

Frozen basket

Done

12 directional city pairs, 24 directed routes, five lead-time strata. Weights from real DGCA city-pair passenger volumes over a trailing twelve months. CI recomputes them from source and checks the file’s SHA-256 on every push.

Index engine

Done

Jevons elementary aggregates, donor imputation, chaining, GEKS multilateral, direct fixed-base, hedonic time-dummy, and revision vintages. Checked against a worked example computed by hand.

Immutable raw layer

Done

Postgres on Supabase, Mumbai region. Append-only is enforced by database rule, not convention — and it was tested by deliberately running UPDATE and DELETE against a row and confirming it survived both untouched.

Collector

Done

robots.txt gate with a logged audit trail, per-domain rate limiting with jitter, exponential backoff, and challenge detection that backs off rather than solving. Writes to Postgres and to CSV in git, every run.

API & dashboard

Done

Versioned FastAPI with a published OpenAPI spec; every value carries its availability rate. Streamlit dashboard with the index band, lead-time curve, route heatmap, availability panel and robustness comparison.

Source verification

Answered

A probe driving a real browser from the Azure US runner found a live fare endpoint returning 2.0 MB of JSON — robots-allowed, no bot wall, real INR fares. The risk that quietly ends most attempts at this problem statement is closed.

Four ways of computing the same number

Differentiator

The honest answer to “why should we trust your index?” is not “trust it”. It is: here are four constructions built on different principles, here is where they disagree, and here is the mechanism that explains the gap.

Series How it is built What it is good at Where it fails
Chained Jevons
Headline		 Day-on-day matched relatives, multiplied Standard CPI construction; revises cleanly; each day’s movement is attributable to that day’s observations Accumulates sampling noise — chain drift
Direct fixed-base Every day compared straight back to day one Immune to drift by construction Matched sample shrinks as the market turns over
GEKS-Jevons Geometric mean of every bilateral path in a rolling window, mean-spliced Transitive — drift has nowhere to accumulate. What Eurostat and the ILO recommend for high-frequency data Computationally heavier; revises the whole window
Hedonic time-dummy Weighted regression on every quote, with fixed effects for route, carrier, lead time, day of week, departure hour and Indian holidays Quality-adjusted; uses the unmatched sample too; comes with a confidence interval Needs volume before it identifies

A bug that turned out to be a finding

Chain drift

The chained index and the hedonic disagreed. They shouldn’t have.

Both were built exactly as specified. Run over a long fixture, they correlated at 0.20 — far worse than two measurements of the same thing should. That is not a coding error. Chaining multiplies daily relatives together, so sampling noise accumulates as a random walk even when prices do not move. It is the central reason official statisticians moved to multilateral index methods for scanner and web-scraped data.

So GEKS-Jevons went in, and the drift is now published as a measured number with its trend per day rather than assumed away. If that gap keeps growing with series length, GEKS gets promoted to headline — and we will see it coming instead of discovering it in December.

These figures come from a labelled test fixture, not from collected fares — the collector has not yet produced a series long enough to diagnose drift. The fixture generator refuses to run without an explicit environment flag, writes only to a gitignored directory, prefixes every row SIMULATED_, and the index builder raises rather than publishing anything sourced from it. Five independent safeguards, because a fabricated number reaching a published series is the worst thing that could happen to this project.

Decisions we can defend

Methodology

A dozen teams will demo a scraper. Almost none will be able to say why their number was computed the way it was. Each of these is a choice, with an alternative that was considered and rejected.

Geometric mean, not arithmetic
Jevons within each stratum — Eurostat’s recommended elementary aggregate for web-scraped data. Base-period invariant, passes time reversal, and damps the downward bias from non-random missingness.
Dutot would let a ₹22,000 last-minute fare dominate a ₹6,000 one. Carli fails time reversal outright — there is a unit test specifically to catch an accidental regression to it.
Three lead-time scenarios, not one weight
Nobody publishes how far in advance Indians book. Rather than invent a booking curve, the index runs under near-term-heavy, uniform and advance-heavy assumptions, and the spread is published as a band.
The band width is itself a finding: where the scenarios agree the headline is robust to what we don’t know; where they diverge, the number rests on an assumption no one has data for.
Sold out and blocked are never merged
A sold-out cell is information about the market — a demand signal the CPI cannot currently produce. A blocked cell is information about us. They are counted, charted and reported separately.
Collapsing them would let our own scraping failures masquerade as market scarcity, which is the single most dangerous thing a price index can do to itself.
Carry-forward is prohibited
Missing strata are imputed by the movement of their nearest observed donor — same route first, then same lead time, then all items. Every imputed cell is flagged with the tier used.
Repeating yesterday’s price asserts zero change on no evidence, and flattens the series exactly when the market is moving most. A day with nothing observed anywhere publishes a gap, not a number.
Revisions are a resource, not a footnote
Every value is stored with its computation timestamp. Recomputing a day creates a new vintage beside the old one; nothing is ever overwritten, and the difference is queryable.
Statistics agencies care about revisions more than almost anything, and no student project implements them.
The DGCA file needed real cleaning
Mumbai appears under two labels in the source and Goa under three. Treat them as separate cities and DEL–BOM’s weight is understated by roughly 40%, while Goa disappears entirely.
The mapping is hand-checked and explicit rather than fuzzy-matched. Unmapped labels are down to 0.7% of traffic, and that residual is reported rather than swallowed.

Collected within hard limits

Scraping policy

Every rule below is enforced in code, and the policy document names the file that enforces each one. When someone asks whether this is legal, the answer should be a query you can run, not a claim on a slide.

Every robots decision is logged
Parsed per domain before every request and written to a queryable table — URL, verdict, reason, crawl-delay. Fail-closed: an unreachable robots.txt means we do not scrape that domain.
We never hide who we are
The User-Agent names the project, links the repository and carries a contact email. No rotation, no residential proxies, no spoofed browser identity.
Slow on purpose
One request per domain every few seconds, randomised, with exponential backoff. We need 120 cells a day, not 120 a minute — speed buys nothing and costs the whole argument.
We never solve a CAPTCHA
A challenge page means the cell is recorded blocked and imputation handles it. Correctly-handled missingness is the answer a statistics agency would give.
Public pages only
No accounts, no logins, no paywall bypass. Every URL is one a member of the public reaches through a site’s own search form.
We didn’t take the shortcut
The probe captured the fare request body, so we could POST that endpoint directly and skip the browser. It carries a session token their own page mints — synthesising it is reverse-engineering an internal API. We load the public page and let their widget call their own endpoint instead.
No personal data, ever
Prices only. That is precisely why the DPDP Act does not attach, and saying so up front pre-empts the question rather than waiting for it.

Six days

Sequence

One constraint shapes the whole ordering: you cannot go back and scrape what a fare was last Tuesday. Every day the collector is not running is a day permanently missing from the end of the series, so it starts on day zero and everything else is built around a clock already ticking.

Day 0

The clock starts Done

Repo public, basket frozen from DGCA data, database live with immutability tested, index engine complete and unit-tested, collector built, API and dashboard running, docs written. Source probe dispatched.

Day 1

Make one source boring Next

EaseMyTrip is the one. Write a precise extractor against its real payload shape, then let the cron fire unattended twice. One source you would bet the month on — not three badly.

Day 2

Second and third sources Planned

Only now. Prefer genuinely independent inventory: a metasearch aggregator plus a direct OTA is two readings; two aggregators reselling one feed is one reading twice.

Day 3

The index meets real fares Planned

Build on real days, read the imputation tiers before interpreting anything, and sanity-check two cells against a manual search — the check no unit test substitutes for.

Day 4

Make it visible Planned

Deploy dashboard and API, pull the CPI air-transport benchmark from eSankhyiki, and get one credible screenshot with real data on it.

Day 5

Evidence and honesty Planned

Back-test on log changes rather than levels. Then parallel manual collection on five cells, proving the collector reads what a human sees — the strongest single piece of validation available.

Day 6

Ship Planned

Twelve slides at three minutes. Two-minute recording ending on the Actions run history — unfakeable evidence that this ran every day without anyone watching. Submit early.

What could still go wrong

Open risks

Written down before being asked. Having a real answer here is worth more than the answer itself.

Answered: one source works, and only one so far

The collector runs on GitHub Actions, whose runners sit in Azure US, and Indian OTAs can behave very differently for a foreign IP than for a consumer in Bengaluru. Probed on day zero rather than week three: EaseMyTrip answers cleanly — robots.txt allows the path, no bot wall, and its booking widget's own endpoint returns 2.0 MB of JSON carrying real INR fares. The collector is viable.

Four other sites — Cleartrip, Yatra, MakeMyTrip and Akasa — return 403 or time out on robots.txt itself. They are not blocking our scraping; they will not serve us their crawl rules. Fail-closed means we leave those domains alone. That rule cost four sources in one afternoon, which is precisely when a rule is worth having.

What remains is concentration risk: one verified source is a single point of failure. ixigo is being re-probed with a longer settle window, and the residual mitigation is the one already built — a blocked cell is recorded as blocked, imputation handles it, and the availability rate makes it visible rather than invisible.

Quoted fares are not transaction prices

The hardest question the panel will ask. We observe what a consumer is offered, not what anyone paid, so we cannot weight a fare by how many seats sold at it.

The CPI already uses offer prices for most of its basket — a shelf price is not a transaction price either. But the limit is real, so the API carries a documented ingestion point for airline MIS data and the weighting layer already accepts an external weight source. Naming the gap and having architected for it is the honest position.

Missingness is not missing-at-random

Sites throttle hardest during high-traffic periods — which are exactly the high-demand periods when fares move most. So our imputation assumes more randomness than reality provides, and the bias runs in a direction that matters.

Mitigated by multiple sources per route and by publishing the availability rate and the directly-observed weight share on every value, so a reader can discount accordingly. Not solved.

Success would break the transparency

If this became official, publishing the basket, the collection window and the methodology would hand carriers a specification to price against. The standard responses — conceal the collection instant, widen the randomisation window, keep the basket confidential, rotate a reserve sample — are configuration changes, not a rewrite, and the randomisation window already exists.

For a research prototype, auditability is worth more than resistance to gaming. An index nobody can check is worth nothing regardless of how un-gameable it is.
